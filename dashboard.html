Test cards

Test 1 — Gate after Q4

Inconclusive
The primary KPI alone looks like a strong win here — that's exactly why the guardrail matters. It isn't apples-to-apples with control's metric, and it moving with the primary number is a red flag, not confirmation.
Primary — gate reached → account created
42% → 55%
Guardrail — drop-off in Q5–Q6 (post-gate)
~12% expected → 21%

The guardrail rises alongside the primary metric — the sunk-cost effect may be masking a flow-break rather than confirming the hypothesis, exactly the risk flagged in the Part 2 write-up. The primary KPI also measures a different funnel span than control's (gate→account vs. full result→account), so it isn't apples-to-apples. Recommend re-measuring as full visit→account CVR before shipping.

Test 2 — Shorter quiz (5 questions)

Kill
Completion rate went up — the primary KPI looks like a clean win on its own. The guardrail (and the net funnel math below) is what shows it isn't.
Primary — quiz start → completion
65% → 77%
Guardrail — result → account created
42% → 33%
72% × 77% × 92% × 33% ≈ 16.8% (vs. 18.1% control baseline)

The guardrail catches exactly what it's for: completion went up, but the "thinner"-feeling result (fewer category data points) hurt conversion enough to make the net funnel worse than control.

Test 3 — Percentile framing

Ship
Primary — result → account created
42% → 47%
Guardrail — time-on-screen before click
14s → 9s
72% × 65% × 92% × 47% ≈ 20.2% (vs. 18.1% control baseline)

Both primary and net CVR improve. The shorter time-on-screen is a secondary watch-item (faster, more impulsive click) worth monitoring post-launch, not a reason to hold the ship decision.
